## 9. Camera field of view (111 deg) and validated-first with the waypoint budget

- **Camera.** The colleague's newer maps carry a horizontal field of view of 111 deg, the older ones 104 deg. The view is read from
  each leaf's stored camera record (`fx`, `width`; `scene_adapter.build_view`), so no constant changes. The view is used only by
  `leafcover.cover` (which leaves to keep for reconstruction and depth); the planner, the close-evidence hold and validated-first do
  not use it. Nothing in the planning path changes with 111 deg.
- **Validated-first and the waypoint budget (bug fixed).** With validated-first on, the lattice route avoids unvalidated space, but the
  waypoint pull judged its chords on free space only. In an L-shaped test (robot drives along two walls of an open room) the old pull
  replaced the validated L by the unvalidated diagonal: 179 unvalidated samples on the polyline, while the route itself reported zero.
  Fixed: `blocked_free_los(..., validated=True)` makes a chord pass only validated cells, when validated-first is on. The graphs
  answer `validated_cell` (engine: `_val`; lazy: the stored `val` plane, True for a store without it). A chord then never adds an
  unvalidated edge the lattice route avoids. Regression test: `tests/unit/scene_graph/test_validated_first.py::test_f_...`.
- **Efficiency.** The validity test is memoised per cell, like the free test; the DP is unchanged (vertex-only, slack in cm).

### 10.3 How free cells are decided today

Free-cell validation means the rule that turns the costmap into the navigable set F. Read it in `packages/scene_graph/engine.py`,
`_build_F` (around line 644) and `_fold_into` (around line 244).

1. **Observation fold.** Each cell keeps its latest value by capture time (max over time, order-independent). A cell with no
   observation has no value and is unknown. The cell value is `V`: `V < 100` means free, `V == 255` means unknown, otherwise blocked.
2. **Free.** `free = (V != 255) & (V < 100)`: known and below the lethal threshold.
3. **Close-evidence hold (rule on).** Two planes are kept: `Fn`, the latest free report from within `near_range_m` (1.3 m), and
   `Bn`, the latest lethal report from within the same range. A cell is held blocked when `(V < 100) & (Bn >= 0) & (Bn > Fn)`,
   so a lethal report from close range wins over an older free one. A lethal report from farther away does not hold; a later
   free report retracts it.
4. **Away-from-walls.** A held component survives only if one of its cells is farther than `near_min_clearance_m` (0.25 m)
   from every blocked or unknown cell. Held cells next to a wall are released.
5. **Erosion.** `_erode` keeps a lattice cell navigable only if a square of side 2 × robot half side (0.5 m) fits in free cells
   at one of 23 headings.
6. **Driven evidence.** The driven polyline of each session (G) and the cells the body covered (W, and P along the chord between
   poses) are added. A cell the body covered is navigable even where the camera never saw it; a cell reported blocked stays
   blocked; along a chord only known-free cells count.
7. **Components.** The connected components of the candidate set that contain driven evidence are F. The charts are the Morse
   partition of F.

Clearance for pricing is the distance to the nearest blocked or unknown cell (`_clearance_cells`), taken as the minimum over each
node's 2x2 costmap cells (`block_min`). Pricing only; it does not change F.

The former validated-first check (a node counts as validated if a close free report exists) is gone. Nothing in the planner
asks whether a cell was seen closely.
